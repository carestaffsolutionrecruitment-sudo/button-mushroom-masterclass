# Standard Operating Procedure: Integrated Pest Management (IPM) & Hygiene Sanitization (SOP-402)

* **Document ID:** SOP-402
* **Version:** 2.0
* **Target Region:** All farm facilities globally, with East African highland seasonal pressure patterns
* **Applies To:** Biosecurity Officers, Farm Workers, Room Supervisors, Visitors
* **Linked Documents:** Module 4 §3 · Deliverable-404 (diagnostic flowchart) · KE-00 §5 (PCPB)

---

## 1. Purpose & Scope

This SOP sets out the biosecurity zoning, hygiene routines, water treatment, pest and disease monitoring, outbreak response, post-crop cook-out, and safe chemical handling required to prevent and remediate pest infestations and pathogen outbreaks.

## 3. Safety & Legal

* Use **only PCPB-registered pest control products**, applied according to the label: crop, dose, pre-harvest interval (PHI), and re-entry interval. Keep a spray record (date, product, PCPB registration number, dose, room, operator, PHI).
* Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated store away from food and packaging; keep Safety Data Sheets on file; never decant into drink bottles.
* PPE per label (gloves, goggles, respirator, overalls). Never mix chlorine products with acids or ammonia (toxic gas).
* Export and supermarket buyers may require residue testing. Prefer non-chemical controls.

## 4. Biosecurity Routines

### 4.1 Zoning and people flow

| Zone | Areas | Overall colour (example) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Clean | Spawning, spawn run, case run, young crops | White |
| Cropping | Rooms in flush, packhouse | Blue |
| Dirty | Compost yard, Phase II, SMC pad, waste | Green |

* Staff move **clean → cropping → dirty** within a day, never back. Assign yard crews to the yard only.
* Visitors: register; no growing-room access if they visited another mushroom farm in the past 48 h; disposable coat and boot covers.

### 4.2 Entry routine (every entry)

1. Footbath: disinfectant at the label rate, **refreshed daily** (more often if soiled; organic matter inactivates disinfectants).
2. Wash hands with soap, then sanitise.
3. Enter via the airlock/double door; close the outer door before opening the inner.

### 4.3 Daily room hygiene

* Remove stumps, trimmings, and diseased mushrooms in **lidded bins** at the end of each picking round. Never leave waste in aisles.
* Clean the packhouse, crates, knives, and picking trays daily (detergent → rinse → disinfect).
* Keep floors drained; no standing water.

### 4.4 Chlorine dilution guide (household/industrial sodium hypochlorite)

> **mL of bleach per litre of water** = Target ppm ÷ (Bleach strength % × 10)

| Use | Target available chlorine | With 3.5% bleach | With 5% bleach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Surfaces, crates, tools (after cleaning) | 500–1,000 ppm | 14–29 mL/L | 10–20 mL/L |
| Floor and drain wash | 1,000 ppm | 29 mL/L | 20 mL/L |
| Casing watering for blotch control | Commonly **100–150 ppm** in extension guidance; start lower (≈ 50 ppm) and watch for cap discolouration | 1.4–4.3 mL/L | 1.0–3.0 mL/L |
| Earth-tube / tank flush | 50 ppm | 1.4 mL/L | 1.0 mL/L |

*Check the strength on your product label. Chlorine decays in sunlight and in dirty water, so make fresh solutions daily and verify with chlorine test strips.*

### 4.5 Exclusion

* ≤ 0.6 mm insect screens on every opening; inspect weekly; repair immediately.
* Doors self-closing; airlocks used; no propped-open doors (even in the hot season).
* Fresh-air filters per SOP-101.
* Compost yard ≥ 30 m from grow rooms and downwind; manure stockpiles covered.
* Ant moats/barriers on shelf legs in passive houses; rodent bait stations **outside** rooms only.

## 5. Monitoring

| Tool | Placement | Frequency | Record |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Yellow sticky traps | 1 per ≈ 25 m² of bed + 1 at each door, at bed height | Count twice weekly; replace when ~50% covered or every 2 weeks | Trap count log (sciarids, phorids, others) |
| UV light trap | Spawning hall, spawn-run corridor | Nightly; empty weekly | Count |
| Room walk-through | Every bed/bag row | Daily | Disease/pest observations, Deliverable-404 Part 2 |
| Compost/casing sampling | Suspect areas | As needed | Larvae, mites, nematodes (Berlese funnel/water extraction if available) |

**Action thresholds (starting points; refine with your own data):**

| Stage | Sciarids per trap per week | Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Spawn run / case run | > 10 | Find the entry point; check screens, doors, and compost; intensify trapping |
| Spawn run / case run | > 50 | Targeted control (registered biological or chemical product), Phase II/casing review |
| Cropping | > 50 | Inspect for larval damage; plan control for the next crop; strict cook-out |

## 6. Seasonal IPM Calendar (Kenyan highlands)

| Period | Elevated risks | Preventive actions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Late Feb – Mar** (before the long rains) | Fly surge as rains start; spore load rising | Replace all screens/filters; re-seal doors; raise the spawn rate to 0.8–1.0%; stock disinfectants |
| **Mar – May** (long rains) | Green mould, blotch, cobweb, wet bubble; flies | Faster cap drying; chlorinated watering; extra trap counts; zero tolerance on door discipline |
| **Jun – Aug** (cool, misty) | Blotch from slow drying; condensation | Anti-condensation baffles; recirculation; harvest-day RH 82–88% |
| **Sep – Oct** (warm, dry) | Phorids; false truffle/thermophilic moulds with spawn-run overheating | Spawn-run temperature control; phorid exclusion |
| **Oct – Dec** (short rains + holiday demand) | As Mar–May, plus pressure to over-crop | Don't extend crops past flush 3 to chase December prices; cook out on schedule |

## 7. Outbreak Response

### 7.1 General rule

**Contain → Remove → Sanitise → Find the root cause → Record.**

### 7.2 Specific responses

| Problem | Immediate response | Root-cause checks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Green mould** (*Trichoderma*) | Spray the spot lightly with water (reduces spore release), cover with damp paper, bag and remove the affected bag/compost section; small spots on casing can be covered with salt | Phase II log (incomplete conditioning?), spawning hygiene, spawn-run overheating |
| **Bacterial blotch** | Increase air movement; drop RH to 82–88%; stop watering before picking; chlorinated watering | Condensation, cap drying time, water quality |
| **Dry bubble** | Cover each spot with salt or a damp paper towel + salt; pick diseased areas **last**; disinfect hands and knives after | Flies (vector), door discipline, cook-out of the previous crop |
| **Wet bubble** | Cover and remove carefully; mark the area; no watering over the spot | Casing pasteurisation log; soil source |
| **Cobweb** | Cover with damp paper then salt; remove; avoid air currents spreading spores | High RH + poor airflow |
| **Sciarid / phorid outbreak** | Increase trapping; find entry points; registered larvicide/biological per label if threshold exceeded | Screens, doors, compost and casing pasteurisation |
| **Mites** | Isolate the room; cook out at crop end | Phase II temperatures; straw/manure source |

## 8. Post-Crop Cook-Out & Room Turn-Around

1. **Cook-out:** with the spent compost still in the room, steam to **65–70 °C compost temperature for 8–12 h** (measured in the compost at the coldest point).
   * *Kenyan low-cost workaround:* if your steam generator cannot reach this in a full room, steam to the maximum achievable, hold ≥ 12 h, and remove the compost in **covered carts** directly to the SMC pad downwind. Record the actual temperatures.
2. Remove the spent compost; it does not go back through any clean area.
3. Wash shelves, walls, floors, and ducts: detergent → rinse → disinfect (§4.4).
4. Dry the room completely with fans before refilling.
5. Replace or wash poly-tube ducts; replace sticky traps.

## 9. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Footbath ineffective (dirty, foamy) | Not refreshed; organic load | Refresh daily; add a boot-brushing station before the bath |
| Repeated disease in the same room | Incomplete cook-out; contaminated shelving/ducts | Verify cook-out temperatures; replace timber with treated/sealed or metal shelving; replace ducts |
| Flies inside despite screens | Gaps at doors, drains, cable entries; screens torn | Smoke-pencil or light test for gaps; seal; drain traps |
| Disease spreading room-to-room | Staff moving backwards through zones; shared tools | Enforce zoning; colour-coded tools per zone |
| Ants raiding shelves | Broken moats; vegetation touching walls | Refill moats; clear a 1 m bare/gravel strip around buildings |
