# Standard Operating Procedure: Phase II Tunnel Pasteurization & Ammonia Clearance (SOP-202)

* **Document ID:** SOP-202
* **Version:** 2.0
* **Target Region:** Global indoor bulk tunnels, with a Kenyan low-cost chamber variant
* **Applies To:** Facility Operators, Climate Technicians, Boiler/Steam Attendants
* **Linked Documents:** Module 2 §4 · SOP-201 · Deliverable-101

---

## 1. Purpose & Scope

This SOP covers loading, levelling, pasteurisation, conditioning (ammonia clearance), and cool-down of Phase I compost in (a) an industrial tunnel or (b) a low-cost insulated chamber heated by an atmospheric biomass steam generator. The output is selective, ammonia-free compost ready for spawning.

> **Sequence:** fill → level → **pasteurise** → **condition** → cool. (Version 1.0 listed conditioning before the peak. Corrected in 2.0.)

---

## 2. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Phase II Operator | Runs the cycle; logs probe readings every 2 h (manual) or verifies controller logs |
| Steam Attendant | Operates the steam generator; never leaves it unattended while fired |
| Compost Manager | Approves release to spawning against the release checklist |

---

## 3. Safety

* **Ammonia:** respirator with an ammonia (K-type) cartridge when entering a tunnel during conditioning; ventilate before entry.
* **Steam and hot compost:** heat-resistant gloves; no entry during the pasteurisation peak.
* **Steam generator (OSHA 2007):** any vessel able to hold pressure must be examined by a DOSHS Approved Person and kept within its certificate. **Use open-vented atmospheric steam generators for low-cost setups**. Never improvise with sealed drums or blocked vents. Keep a water-level sight glass, a free vent, and a trained attendant.
* **Confined space:** tunnels and plenums: permit, gas test (NH₃, O₂), standby person.
* **Electrical:** blower motors earthed and RCD-protected; backup generator transfer switch tested monthly.

## 4. Equipment

| Item | Industrial | Kenyan low-cost variant |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Vessel | Insulated tunnel with slatted/spigot floor | Insulated CSEB or panel chamber (20–30 m³), raised slatted timber/steel floor over a 30–50 cm plenum |
| Air | High-pressure fan, fresh/recirculation dampers | Centrifugal blower (≈ 150–250 m³/h per tonne of compost) with a manual slide damper for fresh air |
| Heat | Steam injection or hot-water coil | Atmospheric biomass steam generator (firewood/briquettes) piped into the plenum |
| Sensors | 4–8 compost probes + air sensors on a controller | 4 digital probe thermometers at fixed points (front/back × top/middle) read every 2 h |
| Ammonia | Electrochemical sensor | Detector tubes (or a handheld meter) and the trained-nose check |
| Backup power | Generator with ATS | Generator sized for the blower **at minimum** |

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Loading (Hour 0)

1. Pre-clean and disinfect the tunnel/chamber after the previous batch; check the floor slots are clear.
2. Load Phase I compost **uniformly** (industrial 1.8–2.5 m depth; low-cost chamber 1.2–1.6 m). Do not compact or walk on the compost. Fill loosely and level.
3. Insert probes at the agreed fixed points (≥ 4).
4. Close doors; start recirculation at a low fresh-air setting.

### 5.2 Stage A — Levelling (≈ 12–24 h)

* Recirculate to equalise the mass at **48–51 °C**; spread between probes ≤ 2 °C.
* Compost's own activity provides most of the heat; add steam only to lift cold spots.

### 5.3 Stage B — Pasteurisation peak (8–12 h)

* Raise the compost core to **58–62 °C** at **all** probe points (steam into the plenum + recirculation).
* Hold for **8–12 h** after the coldest probe reaches 58 °C.
* **Never exceed 63 °C** in the compost. That kills the beneficial thermophiles needed for conditioning.
* Air temperature in the plenum may run 2–5 °C below compost; control on **compost** probes.

### 5.4 Stage C — Conditioning (≈ 3–6 days)

1. Lower to **45–50 °C** (hold ≈ 48 °C) by opening fresh air gradually.
2. Supply enough fresh air to keep compost aerobic: sweet smell, no sourness.
3. Measure air NH₃ in the return/headspace every 12 h:
   * **Milestone:** NH₃ < 150 ppm means conversion is well under way. Continue holding at conditioning temperature.
   * **Release threshold:** NH₃ ≤ 5–10 ppm, **and no ammonia smell** at the compost surface.
4. Look for abundant grey-white fire-fang (actinomycete) flecks throughout.

### 5.5 Stage D — Cool-down (≈ 12–24 h)

* Increase filtered fresh air to bring the compost to **24–26 °C** at all probes.
* At highland sites, **night air** cools fastest and cheapest. Schedule the cool-down overnight.
* Keep the compost covered/closed until spawning to prevent fly entry.

---

## 6. Release Checklist (Compost Manager signs)

* [ ] Peak held at 58–62 °C for ≥ 8 h at **all** probes (log attached)
* [ ] Conditioning milestone < 150 ppm recorded (date/time)
* [ ] Release reading ≤ 5–10 ppm NH₃; no smell
* [ ] Moisture 66–70%; pH 7.2–7.8
* [ ] Colour dark brown; fire-fang flecks present; sweet, bready smell
* [ ] All probes 24–26 °C

---

## 7. Kenyan Operational Notes

* **Power cuts:** a blower stop during the peak or conditioning creates anaerobic zones and hot spots within an hour. Generator start is mandatory; log every outage with duration.
* **Altitude:** atmospheric steam is ≈ 92–93 °C at 2,000–2,300 m. Heat-up is slower than sea-level manuals suggest; start steam earlier.
* **Biomass fuel:** keep ≥ 1.5 batches of dry fuel in stock (wet firewood in the rains produces little steam); log fuel use per batch to track efficiency.
* **Ammonia tubes are costly:** use them at the milestone and at release; in between, use trained-nose checks and pH strips on condensate.

## 8. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cannot reach 58 °C at some probes | Uneven fill; compaction; blocked floor slots; weak steam | Check the fill and slots before the next batch; increase recirculation; extend steam; extend the hold until the cold probe has 8 h ≥ 58 °C |
| Compost overshoots > 63 °C | Too much steam; too little fresh air | Cut steam; open fresh air; log the event (expect slower conditioning) |
| Ammonia stays > 150 ppm after 4 days | Too much N in the recipe; anaerobic zones; conditioning temperature too high (> 52 °C) or too low (< 44 °C) | Hold at 47–49 °C; increase fresh air; check for wet compacted zones; reduce urea/manure in the next batch (Deliverable-202) |
| Sour/vinegar smell | Anaerobic: too wet, too dense, insufficient air | More fresh air; next batch: drier Phase I end (≤ 74%), looser fill |
| Greasy, dark, sticky compost | Over-wet; under-gypsumed; over-composted in Phase I | Adjust Phase I; confirm gypsum dose |
| No fire-fang flecks | Pasteurisation too hot (> 63 °C); conditioning too short or too cool | Control peak temperature; hold conditioning at 48 °C longer |
| Pests found after Phase II (mites, fly larvae) | Cold spots during the peak; reinfestation during cool-down | Fix uniformity; screen the fresh-air inlet; keep compost covered after cool-down |

## 9. Quality Control Audit

* [ ] Ammonia milestone **< 150 ppm** and release **≤ 5–10 ppm** recorded (tube readings or sensor log)
* [ ] Pasteurisation **58–62 °C for ≥ 8 h** at all probes
* [ ] No compost reading > 63 °C
* [ ] Final compost **24–26 °C**
* [ ] Outage log, fuel log, steam generator checks complete
