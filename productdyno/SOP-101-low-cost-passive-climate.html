Standard Operating Procedure: Low-Cost Passive Climate Control & Insulation Setup (SOP-101)

  • Document ID: SOP-101
  • Version: 2.0
  • Target Region: Kenya & East African highlands (primary); tropical highland sites worldwide
  • Applies To: Farm Managers, Infrastructure Builders, Growing-Room Supervisors
  • Linked Documents: Module 1 · Deliverable-101 · KE-00 §1, §3, §4

1. Purpose & Scope

This SOP covers (a) the build-quality checks for a passive or low-energy growing house (Track K1) and (b) the daily operation of its passive climate systems (thermal mass, night-flush ventilation, earth tubes, evaporative curtains, humidity control) so that rooms hold the parameters in Deliverable-101 without industrial HVAC.

2. Responsibilities

Role Responsibility
Farm Manager Approves the vent schedule for each season; reviews logger data weekly
Growing-Room Supervisor Executes the daily schedule; records readings; escalates alarms
Maintenance Technician Screens, filters, earth-tube drains, fans, door seals, gutters

3. Safety

  • Working at height (roof, thatch, gable vents): ladder footed by a second person; harness on roofs over 2 m.
  • Electrical: all fans and heaters on RCD-protected circuits; no extension leads on wet floors.
  • Combustion heaters prohibited in growing rooms (carbon monoxide risk to staff; combustion gases damage the crop).
  • Thatch fire safety: no smoking within 10 m; extinguisher (dry powder + water) at each thatched building.

5. Part B — Daily Passive Climate Operation

5.1 Readings (every day)

Record on the room log (Deliverable-303 during spawn run; room log or Deliverable-404 during cropping): * 06:00–08:00 (coldest point) and 14:00–16:00 (hottest point): air temperature, RH, compost/bed temperature, CO₂ (altitude-corrected, KE-00 §1.4). * Reset the min/max thermometer after each morning reading.

5.2 Seasonal vent schedules (starting points — tune to your logger data)

Fruiting rooms (target air 16–18 °C, RH 85–90%, CO₂ < 1,000 ppm):

Season 20:00 – 07:00 (night) 07:00 – 11:00 11:00 – 16:00 (peak heat) 16:00 – 20:00
Jan–Feb, Sep–Oct (hot/dry) Vents fully open (night flush); earth tubes on Vents ½ open; wet curtains on Vents throttled to the minimum for CO₂ < 1,000 ppm; curtains wet; earth tubes on Open progressively as outside air drops below room air
Mar–May, Oct–Dec (rains) Vents open; watch for over-humid air Vents ½–¾ open; recirculation fans on to dry caps Vents ½ open; curtains off (little evaporative effect and mould risk) Vents open
Jun–Aug (cool) Vents throttled (prevent < 15 °C) Vents open once outside air ≥ 14 °C Vents open (free cooling and CO₂ purge) Throttle back

Spawn-run rooms (compost 24–25 °C, high CO₂): keep vents closed; recirculation only. Open briefly at night only if compost exceeds 26 °C. On cold nights (Jun–Aug, high sites), run the thermostatic heater if room air falls below 19 °C.

Golden rule: bring outside air in only when it is cooler than the room air (for cooling) or when CO₂ must be purged. Check with the outdoor thermometer, not the clock.

5.3 Evaporative cooling curtains

  1. Fill the elevated tank; open the drip line to the curtains/pads until they are uniformly wet with no dripping onto the floor beyond the gutter.
  2. Use only in the dry seasons (wet-bulb depression ≥ 4 °C).
  3. Keep two sets; swap weekly; sun-dry and brush the off-duty set; replace hessian monthly or at the first sign of mould.

5.4 Humidity control

  • Raise RH: knapsack or low-pressure misting of walls, floors, and aisles, never directly onto pins; wet floor channels; humidifier where fitted.
  • Lower RH (rainy seasons): increase recirculation; open vents during the warmest, driest part of the day; avoid floor wetting; reduce watering on harvest days.

5.5 Weekly maintenance

  • ☐ Brush and wash insect screens; repair any hole immediately
  • ☐ Clean or replace intake filters
  • ☐ Check earth-tube drains and flush with chlorinated water (≈ 50 ppm) monthly
  • ☐ Inspect door gaskets and vent shutters
  • ☐ Clear gutters; check roof for leaks before each rainy season

7. Troubleshooting

Symptom Likely cause Corrective action
Fruiting room > 19 °C every afternoon Roof heat gain; vents open in heat; insufficient night flush Throttle vents 11:00–16:00; extend the night flush; check roof insulation and the radiant-barrier air gap; add earth tubes or shade gables
Room < 14 °C at dawn Vents open on cold nights; poor door seals Throttle night vents; fix gaskets; insulate doors
Daily swing > 4 °C inside Insufficient thermal mass/insulation; uncontrolled infiltration Seal gaps; insulate the roof first; keep doors shut; use the airlock
CO₂ > 1,500 ppm during fruiting Under-ventilated; stack effect too weak Open vents; add an inline fan to earth tubes; increase vent height difference
RH < 80%, caps scaling/cracking Dry season; over-ventilation Mist walls/floor; wet curtains; reduce fresh air while keeping CO₂ < 1,000 ppm
RH > 95%, droplets on caps Rainy season; poor air movement Recirculation fans; vent in the warmest hours; reduce watering
Mould on hessian curtains Continuous wetness, no rotation Rotate weekly; sun-dry; replace; stop curtains in the rains
Condensation dripping from the ceiling Missing/punctured vapour barrier; cold ceiling Repair the barrier; add insulation; fit drip baffles over shelves
Earth-tube air smells musty Blocked condensate drain; standing water Clear the drain; chlorine flush; check the fall

8. Quality Control & Daily Audit Checklist

  • ☐ Readings logged at the coldest and hottest points of the day
  • ☐ Fruiting air temperature within 16–18 °C (spawn-run compost 24–25 °C)
  • ☐ CO₂ reading altitude-corrected and within target
  • ☐ Vent positions match the seasonal schedule (or deviation recorded with a reason)
  • ☐ Curtains moist and free of mould (dry seasons only)
  • ☐ Floor channels wetted if RH < 85% (fruiting)
  • ☐ Screens intact; doors closed; airlock in use
