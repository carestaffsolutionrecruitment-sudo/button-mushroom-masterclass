Standard Operating Procedure: Gentle Harvesting, Quality Grading, & Cold-Storage (SOP-403)

  • Document ID: SOP-403
  • Version: 2.0
  • Target Region: Global harvesting and packing rooms, with Kenyan retail, HoReCa, and cold-chain specifics
  • Applies To: Harvesters, Quality Control Inspectors, Packers, Drivers
  • Linked Documents: Module 4 §4–5 · Deliverable-404 · KE-00 §5 (labelling, KEBS, food safety)

1. Purpose & Scope

This SOP defines harvesting technique, timing, grading, packing, labelling, cooling, cold storage, and dispatch so that product reaches buyers at maximum quality and shelf life.

2. Responsibilities

Role Responsibility
Harvest Supervisor Picking plan per room; crew hygiene; yield recording
Harvesters Picking and field grading per this SOP
QC Inspector Grade checks, weight checks, cold-room temperature, release
Packers Packing, labelling, cold storage
Driver Pre-cooled vehicle/coolers, delivery temperature, eTIMS delivery documents

3. Hygiene & Safety

  • Medical certificates for all food handlers (county public health requirement); clean overalls, hair cover, no jewellery; short clean fingernails.
  • Handwashing before starting, after breaks, and after the toilet; gloves where buyer specs require them.
  • Knives sanitised at the start of each room and after diseased patches.
  • Pick diseased areas last and put the waste in lidded bins; never mix with product.
  • Ergonomics: rotate pickers between tiers; use stable platforms for upper tiers (no climbing on shelves).
  • Cold room: inside-release door handle; never lock staff in; limit continuous time in the cold room and provide warm clothing.

4. Procedure

4.1 Picking plan

  • Pick daily, starting at the coolest hour (≈ 06:00), cleanest rooms first (youngest crop → oldest crop).
  • Peak flush days: pick twice daily (morning + early afternoon).
  • Pick mushrooms before the veil stretches for button grade; leave developing pins undisturbed.

4.2 Technique

  1. Grip the cap gently with the fingertips; twist and lift. On dense clusters, support the neighbours with the other hand.
  2. Trim the stem base with a sharp knife over the picking tray, not over the bed (soil and casing on the bed spread disease).
  3. Stem length per buyer spec (typically 10–15 mm below the cap for retail).
  4. Pick-to-pack where possible: harvest Grade A straight into the final punnet or crate to avoid re-handling bruises.
  5. Never throw or drop mushrooms; trays no more than 2–3 layers deep.
  6. Fill holes left in the casing and level disturbed casing after picking.
  7. Water after the picking round, never before.

4.3 Grading standards

Grade Criteria Cap diameter Destination
A — Button Closed cap, veil intact, firm, white/clean; no marks 25–45 mm (buyer spec) Supermarket punnets, premium HoReCa
B — Cup Veil partly stretched/just open; firm; minor marks 35–55 mm HoReCa, wholesale, loose retail
C — Flats/Open Fully open, dark gills, or minor bruising 50–90 mm+ Open-air markets, drying/processing
Reject Diseased (blotch, bubble, cobweb), insect-damaged, slimy Any Destroy in lidded waste; never sell

International reference: UNECE FFV-24 (cultivated mushrooms). The buyer's written specification overrides this table. File a signed spec sheet for every buyer.

4.4 Packing & labelling

  • Pack in the cold room or a cool packing area (≤ 12 °C if possible) with pre-cooled packaging.
  • Retail punnets (200 g / 250 g): check-weigh (minimum declared net weight; no short weights), micro-perforated film.
  • Label (minimum): product name ("Fresh Button Mushrooms Agaricus bisporus"), net weight, producer name and physical address, pack date and best-before (e.g., +5 days at 2–4 °C), batch/room code, storage instruction ("Keep refrigerated at 2–4 °C"), and barcode where the retailer requires it (GS1 Kenya).
  • HoReCa crates (2–5 kg): paper-lined ventilated crates; crate label with grade, weight, and date.

4.5 Cooling & cold storage

  1. Move picked trays to the cold room within 30 minutes of each picking round; never leave trays in the growing room or in the sun.
  2. Cool to 2–4 °C core, ≈ 90% RH within ≈ 2–4 h (industrial vacuum cooling: ≈ 15–30 min).
  3. Forced-air cooling: arrange crates in rows with a fan drawing air through them; do not block evaporator airflow.
  4. Record the cold-room temperature at least 3× daily (or a logger); alarm if > 6 °C.
  5. FIFO: oldest stock dispatched first; maximum cold-store holding ≤ 48 h before dispatch for retail.

4.6 Dispatch

  • Pre-cool the vehicle box or coolers; frozen gel packs wrapped (never touching product).
  • Dispatch early morning; record product temperature at loading and at delivery on the delivery note.
  • Issue an eTIMS invoice for every commercial delivery.
  • Returns/rejections: record the reason; feed the data back into grading and cold-chain reviews.

5. Kenyan Cold-Chain Workarounds

Situation Workaround
Power outage Keep the cold-room door closed; the generator/solar backup starts; water-jug thermal mass in the room; log the outage
No refrigerated vehicle Insulated cooler boxes + gel packs; refrigerated van hire for DC runs; deliver before 10:00
Long route (Eldoret / Nyeri → Nairobi) Pre-dawn or overnight departure; product pre-cooled to 2 °C; reefer courier
Market day without a cold room Evaporative charcoal cooler or wet-sack shading for < 24 h; sell Grade B/C first
Condensation on punnets Pack inside the cold room; avoid warm–cold cycling; micro-perforated film

6. Troubleshooting

Symptom Likely cause Corrective action
Brown bruising after packing Rough handling; re-handling; warm product packed Pick-to-pack; train pickers; pre-cool before packing
Caps open in the cold room Picked too mature; slow cooling Pick earlier; twice-daily picking at peak; faster cooling
Slimy caps / blotch at the buyer Wet caps at picking; condensation; cold chain broken No watering before picking; dry caps; continuous cold chain
Short shelf life (< 3 days) Field heat not removed; temperature abuse in transit Cool within 2–4 h; log delivery temperatures; insulated transport
Underweight punnets rejected No check-weighing Calibrated scale; check every punnet
Supermarket rejection for spec Size or colour outside spec Grade-out at picking; train using photographic spec cards
Stems dirty with casing Trimming over the bed; casing too wet Trim over the tray; adjust watering

7. Quality Control Audit

  • ☐ Harvesters follow clean-hands, glove, and knife hygiene policies
  • ☐ Graded batches match grade and size specifications (spot-check 20 units per batch)
  • ☐ Punnets check-weighed; labels complete and correct
  • ☐ Cold room verified at 2–4 °C before packing and 3× daily
  • ☐ Product at ≤ 4 °C at dispatch; delivery temperature recorded
  • ☐ Yields recorded by room, flush, and grade on Deliverable-404
  • ☐ eTIMS invoices issued for every delivery
