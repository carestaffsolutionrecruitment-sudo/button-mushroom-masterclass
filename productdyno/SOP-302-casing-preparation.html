Standard Operating Procedure: Casing Layer Preparation, Pasteurization, & Application (SOP-302)

  • Document ID: SOP-302
  • Version: 2.0
  • Target Region: Global cultivation facilities, with Kenyan local-material formulations
  • Applies To: Casing Preparation Technicians, Growers, Room Supervisors
  • Linked Documents: Module 3 §5 · Deliverable-101 · KE-00 §2.2, §3.4

1. Purpose & Scope

This SOP covers sourcing, formulation, pH buffering (lime jar test), pasteurisation, moisture adjustment, application, case-run management, and ruffling of the casing layer for Agaricus bisporus.

2. Responsibilities

Role Responsibility
Casing Technician Mixing, jar tests, pasteurisation, moisture adjustment
Room Supervisor Application depth, watering-in, case-run monitoring, ruffling
Farm Manager Approves new casing material sources

3. Safety

  • Dust masks (FFP2) when handling dry peat, coir, lime, or soil.
  • Agricultural lime is mildly caustic in the eyes. Wear goggles while mixing.
  • Steam pasteurisation: follow SOP-202 §3 steam-generator rules.
  • Soil excavation: shore any pit deeper than 1.2 m; never undercut banks.

4. Materials

Material Specification
Coco peat / coir Washed and buffered, EC < 1.0 mS/cm; from the coast (Kilifi/Kwale) or imported
Red volcanic subsoil / sandy loam Dug from 40–80 cm depth (below the topsoil) on your own land or from a licensed supplier; sieved ≤ 10 mm; no black cotton soil
Sphagnum peat (premium) Imported, graded
Weathered SMC (optional) ≥ 6 months weathered; ≤ 15–30% of the mix; check EC
Agricultural limestone (CaCO₃) Fine grade (≥ 80% passing 0.25 mm)
Water pH 6.5–7.8, EC < 1.0 mS/cm, low iron (KE-00 §3.4)

Prohibited: material from gazetted forests, wetlands, or papyrus/peat swamps (illegal); unpasteurised topsoil; black cotton soil.

Coir washing workaround: soak coir blocks in clean water for 12–24 h, drain, and repeat until run-off EC < 1.0 mS/cm (usually 2–3 rinses). If the coir is unbuffered, a final soak in calcium nitrate solution per the supplier's recommendation helps displace sodium/potassium.

5. Procedure

5.1 Formulation (volume basis)

Code Mix Starting lime rate (confirm by jar test)
CS-KE1 50% buffered coir + 50% red subsoil 15–25 kg/m³
CS-KE2 60% sandy loam + 25% coir + 15% weathered SMC 10–20 kg/m³
CS-KE3 60% sphagnum peat + 40% buffered coir 25–40 kg/m³
CS-GL Commercial peat casing Supplier specification

Quantity: bed area (m²) × depth (m) × 1.1 (settling allowance). Example: 100 m² × 0.045 m × 1.1 ≈ 5.0 m³ (budget 4.5 m³ of settled casing in Deliverable-505).

5.2 Lime jar test (every new material lot)

  1. Five 1-litre moist samples; lime at 0 / 10 / 20 / 30 / 40 g per litre (= kg/m³).
  2. Mix, moisten, cover; wait 48–72 h.
  3. pH in a 1:5 casing:distilled water suspension, after 30 min settling, with a calibrated meter (calibrate at pH 7 and 10 on the day of use).
  4. Select the lowest rate giving pH 7.2–7.5; add a ≈ 10% reserve.
  5. Using high-bicarbonate borehole water for watering? Use the lower end of the range.

Workaround if no pH meter: universal indicator strips are acceptable for screening but must be confirmed with a meter (borrowed, or at a county/university soil lab) for each new material.

5.3 Mixing

  1. Mix dry or semi-moist components on a clean, disinfected slab.
  2. Add lime evenly in three portions, turning after each.
  3. Add water gradually to near field capacity (squeeze: holds shape, glistens, 1–2 drops).

5.4 Pasteurisation

  1. Load the casing loosely (≤ 60 cm deep) into the Phase II chamber between compost batches, or into a dedicated steam box with a perforated floor.
  2. Steam until all probe points reach 60–65 °C; hold 4–6 h.
  3. Do not exceed 70 °C for long (that destroys beneficial bacteria and can release manganese/ammonium from soil components).
  4. Cool with filtered air; cover; store in the clean zone; use within 3–5 days.

5.5 Application (on fully colonised compost)

  1. Level the compost surface; remove any contaminated patches first.
  2. Apply casing to a uniform 3–5 cm; check depth with a marked stick at 5 points per bag-row/shelf.
  3. Water in over 2–3 days with a fine rose (≈ 1–2 L/m² per application) until the casing reaches field capacity. Do not let water pass into the compost.

5.6 Case run (≈ 7–10 days)

  • Air 22–24 °C; compost 23–25 °C; RH 90–95%; CO₂ 3,000–5,000 ppm.
  • Ruffling: around Day 5–7, when mycelium has grown about two-thirds of the way through the casing, rake the top 2–3 cm with a clean hand rake or fork to spread mycelium evenly. Disinfect rakes between rooms.
  • Move to pinning (SOP-401) when mycelium shows at the surface over ≈ 70% of the area.

6. Troubleshooting

Symptom Likely cause Corrective action
pH drifts below 7.0 during cropping Under-limed; acidic rainwater; acidic subsoil Increase the lime reserve next batch; light lime-water spray (consult your advisor)
pH > 8.0 Over-liming; high-bicarbonate water Reduce lime; acidify or blend the watering water (KE-00 §3.4)
Casing crusts/seals after watering Too much fine soil; clay subsoil; heavy watering Increase the coir/peat share; water with a fine rose; less per application
Casing dries fast, mycelium won't grow up into it Low water-holding; too sandy More coir/peat; check the RH
Mycelium overgrows the surface ("stroma") Case run too long; CO₂ too high; too warm Ruffle; start the pinning shock earlier
Wet bubble (Mycogone) Soil casing inadequately pasteurised Verify 60–65 °C for 4–6 h at all probe points; never use field topsoil
Salt damage / poor colonisation High EC coir or SMC Wash coir; reduce SMC; test EC
Uneven pinning later Uneven casing depth or moisture Depth checks at application; consistent watering

7. Quality Control Audit

  • ☐ Jar test record for this material lot; pH 7.2–7.5 verified by meter
  • ☐ EC < 1.0 mS/cm (coir/SMC components)
  • ☐ Pasteurisation 60–65 °C for 4–6 h at all probes, logged
  • ☐ Casing moisture at field capacity before application
  • ☐ Uniform 3–5 cm depth (5-point checks recorded)
  • ☐ Ruffling date and case-run conditions recorded
