# Module 2: Substrate Engineering: Composting & Pasteurization (Phase I & Phase II)

## 1. Raw Material Formulation
The foundation of successful button mushroom (*Agaricus bisporus*) cultivation relies on a chemically and physically balanced substrate. Unlike wood-rotting mushrooms, button mushrooms require a selectively fermented compost.

* **Carbon-to-Nitrogen ($C:N$) Ratio:** 
  * Target a starting $C:N$ ratio of approximately 30:1, which gradually breaks down and concentrates down to a final ratio of 20:1 post-composting.
* **Core Formulation Components:**
  * **Base Material:** Wheat or barley straw supplying structural carbon and cellulose.
  * **Animal Manures:** Horse or poultry manure providing organic nitrogen and microbial inoculants.
  * **Chemical Amendments:** Agricultural gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4$) added to flocculate the compost, maintain physical structure, and prevent greasiness; urea or ammonium nitrate supplements to balance nitrogen levels.

---

## 2. Phase I Composting (Outdoor/Yard Fermentation)
Phase I is an outdoor, aerobic/anaerobic solid-state fermentation process designed to initiate physical softening and chemical bonding of raw ingredients.

* **The Turning & Management Schedule:** 
  * Executed on a strict timeline: **Day 0** (Initial stacking, pre-wetting, and building the stack), **Day 4** (First turn and water adjustment), **Day 7** (Second turn, adding gypsum), **Day 10** (Third turn), and **Day 13** (Fourth turn / final preparation for Phase II).
* **Key Environmental Targets:**
  * **Moisture Retention:** Maintain moisture levels strictly between $70\% - 75\%$.
  * **Temperature Spikes:** Natural thermophilic microbial activity drives internal stack temperatures up to $65^\circ\text{C} - 80^\circ\text{C}$, softening the straw and transforming harsh raw materials into a uniform matrix.

---

## 3. Phase II Pasteurization & Conditioning (Indoor Bulking Tunnels)
Phase II moves the compost into controlled indoor tunnels or cabinets to eradicate pests and pathogens while converting remaining free ammonia into microbial protein.

* **Stage A: Leveling Stage:**
  * Equalize mass temperature across the entire tunnel bed to a uniform $48^\circ\text{C} - 51^\circ\text{C}$ using automated fan recirculation and fresh air integration.
* **Stage B: Conditioning Stage:**
  * Maintain a steady temperature range of $48^\circ\text{C} - 52^\circ\text{C}$ during this metabolic window. Thermophilic actinomycetes ("fire fangs") convert residual ammonia until ambient gaseous ammonia levels drop below $150\text{ ppm}$.
* **Stage C: Pasteurization Spike:**
  * Raise the compost core temperature to $58^\circ\text{C} - 62^\circ\text{C}$ for a minimum of $8 - 12$ hours. This eliminates nematodes, insect larvae, and competitor molds while preserving beneficial thermophiles.
* **Cool-Down Phase:**
  * Rapidly and controllably drop the compost temperature down to $24^\circ\text{C} - 26^\circ\text{C}$, preparing the substrate for immediate spawning.
