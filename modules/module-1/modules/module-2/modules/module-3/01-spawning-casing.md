# Module 3: Spawning, Spawn Run, & Casing Layer Management

## 1. Inoculation & Grain Spawn Handling
Once pasteurized compost is cooled to target temperatures, the biological inoculation phase begins.

* **Strain Selection:** 
  * Select vigorous, disease-resistant *Agaricus bisporus* hybrid strains (e.g., commercial white U1 lines, hybrid brown, or cream lines tailored to target markets).
* **Inoculation Rates & Blending:** 
  * Thoroughly mix grain spawn into the compost at a rate of $0.5\% - 1.0\%$ based on the wet weight of the substrate.
* **Sanitation Protocols:** 
  * Maintain clean, sanitized room conditions (disinfected surfaces, filtered air handling, and strict hygiene protocols) during bulk bag filling or Dutch tray loading to prevent early contamination.

---

## 2. Colonization (Spawn Run Optimization)
The spawn run phase governs how rapidly and completely the mycelium colonizes the nutrient matrix.

* **Thermal & Environmental Control:** 
  * Maintain compost internal temperature between $24^\circ\text{C} - 25^\circ\text{C}$. Strictly monitor that internal block temperatures never exceed a maximum threshold of $27^\circ\text{C}$, which causes irreversible mycelial thermal death.
* **$\text{CO}_2$ Management:** 
  * Keep carbon dioxide concentrations elevated ($5,000 - 10,000\text{ ppm}$) throughout the colonization period. High $\text{CO}_2$ accelerates vegetative hyphal growth and prevents premature pinhead formation before the substrate is fully consumed.

---

## 3. Casing Layer Science & Application
A casing layer is mandatory for *Agaricus bisporus* because mycelium will not fruit directly on raw compost.

* **Formulation & pH Adjustment:** 
  * Prepare a structurally stable, highly water-retentive casing medium using sphagnum peated moss, neutralized spent mushroom compost, or treated loam/sand blends. 
  * Adjust and buffer the mixture to a slightly alkaline $\text{pH}$ range of $7.2 - 7.5$ using agricultural limestone ($\mathrm{CaCO}_3$).
* **Application & Ruffling Mechanics:** 
  * Apply a uniform 3–5 cm layer over the fully colonized compost. 
  * Execute ruffling/scratching techniques (intermixing the upper casing grains with colonized compost) after the mycelium knits into the casing layer to stimulate uniform pinhead development. Maintain optimal moisture without water-logging the substrate underneath.
